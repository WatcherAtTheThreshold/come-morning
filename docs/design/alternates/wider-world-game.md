## The idea, as described

Come Morning's town, crossed with `greenhorn`'s world and the Ash prose, starts to
suggest something Zelda-shaped:

- You **explore to gather** — mushrooms and whatever else the Kithrin want
- The places you gather in are **dangerous**, so gathering costs more than time
- What is out there varies by fiction: giant arthropods (Green Horn), forest
  creatures, trolls and goblins, or Mushes and Slimes as a Threshold Deep throwback
- You come back to a town that visibly remembers what you brought it

The town half of that already exists and works. The exploration half is entirely
unbuilt, and the fighting half is the part that contradicts the project it would
be built on.

## Why it is not a Come Morning milestone

Come Morning answers *how does it feel when a place gets better because you kept
showing up?* Adding combat does not extend that question, it replaces it — the
game becomes about the trip, and the town becomes the shop you visit between
trips. That is a good game. It is a different one, and it is the one every other
project in the account already is.

The narrower reason: milestone 1 is the second Kithrin, and it exists to make
scarcity into a choice. A dangerous gathering area makes resources scarce through
*risk* instead, which is a different and much louder answer to the same problem.
Building it first would mean never finding out whether the quiet answer worked.

## What Come Morning should take from it anyway

Three things here are compatible with the no-combat constraint and worth stealing:

**Gathering as a place you go, not a menu.** The open question in `milestone-0.md`
— *where do resources come from?* — has no design yet. "A place you travel to,
with something to notice in it" is a better answer than a harvest button, and it
needs no combat to be one.

**Danger is not the only pressure.** Weather, distance, the day running out, a
route that closes at dusk until a lantern exists somewhere. `town-outline.md`
already contributed *the scarce resource is the day, not clicks*.

**The giving is a verb.** Milestone 0's accidental discovery — placing mushrooms
on the counter is a pleasure on its own — suggests the loop can carry more weight
on the town side than the design assumed.

# The shared world, and where these stories sit

The world material below is **not** parked — it is real, it spans several projects,
and its canonical home is `greenhorn/docs/shared-world.md`, which already has a
timeline. This section exists so the Smallish Realms material is written down
somewhere rather than nowhere.

## Smallish Realms

An unfinished sidescroller built in GDevelop, before the move to Godot. Its story:

You play **Magdaline**, searching for your friend **Timothy**. You reach his house
and he is not there. A key under a rock lets you in. Inside: no Timothy, but a
strange container and a receipt from a local store.

On the road to town you meet the **Sullen Traveler**. You ask whether he has seen
your friend, and mention that he is a robot. *Robots don't last long in the wilds.*

In town, the **trader** — strange, with a strange cat — tells you in riddles that
he has seen Timothy, and which way he was heading.

**Title card. You are Timothy.** He walks, glitching and buzzing and popping. He
meets **Fizwik**, a local robot mechanic. Timothy glitches out and falls — and now
you are playing as Fizwik, who walks to him and promises to get him back to the
shop.

**Back to Magdaline**, looking for the cave the trader described. She spots it, and
the Sullen Traveler ahead of it, holding a glowing crystal she last saw in
Timothy's house. She confronts him. He kicks her down into the cave.

She falls ten or fifteen feet onto mossy ground, and explores: more glowing
crystals, ancient machinery, and the question of why Timothy would ever have been
down here. Further in, dormant, lies **the Cave Titan** — a giant robot, who stirs
as she approaches and seems to know her, or to know she was coming. He tells her
where to look next.

Demo ends.

Character art exists for the Cave Titan, the trader, the trader's cat, the Sullen
Traveler, and full turnarounds for Timothy, Magdaline and Fizwik.

## Why this is not a separate universe

`greenhorn/docs/shared-world.md` establishes one clock: humans build a
photosynthesis fix, it overshoots to 35% oxygen, humans die, arthropods grow huge,
robot technicians run a plan to bring the number down — and thousands of years
later the re-seeding has worked, there are villages and Kithren, and the surviving
technicians still walk their routes.

Timothy is in that document already, as the clearest expression of its central
idea: *a technician, thousands of reactivations later, running a copy of a copy of
a copy.* Ash 3 gives him the line — *"every fix and rewrite is a chance for
further corruption."*

**Smallish Realms is that same degradation, dramatised as an inciting incident.**
Timothy glitching on a road and falling is not a similar idea; it is the shared
world's stated theme, happening to the character the doc already names. Fizwik
carrying him to a workshop is the maintenance the doc says these machines depend
on. The Cave Titan asleep among ancient machinery and glowing crystals sits
naturally among the failing nodes.

So the clock likely reads:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The overshoot** | humans build the fix, it works too well, they die |
| **Green Horn** | robots execute the plan among giant arthropods |
| *…thousands of years, nodes failing, copies of copies…* | |
| **Come Morning** | Kithrin villages. A place gets better because someone kept showing up |
| **Ash** | Ash, Tilly, Timothy, the Gloam Knight |
| **Smallish Realms** | Magdaline and Timothy. Where Come Morning's Kithrin and Ash's technicians share a road |

The order of the last three is unsettled and is a real question, not a formatting
one. Come Morning has Kithrin running market stalls; `shared-world.md` lists *"do
the Kithren exist during Green Horn?"* as open. Whether Come Morning sits before,
during or after the Ash stories decides what Tilly knows about robots.

## The long slope from cataclysm to present day

The idea Jessop most wanted recorded: **that it should take a very long time to get
from the cataclysm to the "present day" of these stories.**

That is already the shape of `shared-world.md`'s clock, and it is what makes the
material work. The distance is what lets Green Horn be grim without being bleak —
the re-seeding does succeed, there are chickadees, thousands of years downstream —
and it is what lets Come Morning be gentle without being naive, because the town
is standing on top of an extinction nobody in it remembers.

It also means these projects do not compete. They are not versions of one game.
They are widely separated points on one clock, and a thing established in any of
them costs the others nothing.

## Unfinished

- **The Gloam Knight story.** Introduced in `ash-3`, unfinished
- **The knight class** — a living pantheon of legends, of whom the Gloam Knight is
  a dark one
- **Timothy and Magdaline**, intended as a love story where neither understands why
  they feel as they do about each other. Given what the shared world says about
  copied memory and corruption, that not-knowing may not be a metaphor
